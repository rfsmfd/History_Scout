What History Scout is

A field notebook for old places. You photograph a homestead, church, school, cemetery, archaeological site, mill, old place or store; the app records where you stood, which way you faced, and when — then gets out of your way. The writing up happens later, at the kitchen table, on whichever device you have to hand.

It works in two halves:
The phone, in the fieldPhotograph with your iPhone's own camera — full size, zoom, flash — then bring the pictures in. History Scout files each one by where it was taken. No signal needed.
The computer, at the deskThe same records on a big screen with a keyboard, photos filed into folders on your drive, and lists ready for ArcGIS.
ArcGIS, at the endOne master project holds every site and every photograph for good. A tool brings each round of field work in, and never overwrites what you have done there yourself — see section 10.

Everything you record is kept on the device you recorded it on and, once you sign in, in your own private online store. Nothing is shared with anyone.

It is a web app. There is nothing to install from a store: you open a web address, and on the phone you add it to the home screen so it behaves like any other app. Updates arrive by themselves; you tap a banner.

  1. Tap Sign in.
  2. Type your email and password.
  3. Tap the Sign in button underneath.
  4. You land on the Capture screen. Near the top it should say ☁ Records backed up.

  1. Tap Sign in, type your email.
  2. Tap Forgot password?
  3. Google emails you a link to set a new one. It may land in your Spam folder.

2 · The Capture screen — recording in the field

This screen is built for gloves, sunlight and no signal. Everything else in the app can wait.

What is on it

  • Position — your coordinates and how accurate they are. Green is good, amber is fair, red means no fix yet.
  • Heading — which way the phone is pointing. Tap it to open the compass.
  • Local time — what goes on the record.
  • The bar — says where the next photo will land: a site you're already working on, or a new one.
  • 🖼 From Library — the big button, on the side of the hand you hold the phone in (right, unless you choose left in Capture Settings). It brings in photos you took with the iPhone's Camera app at full size, with their location — see Photos brought in from the library below.
  • 📷 Capture Site — a quick shot straight into History Scout. Handy for one picture at a site you are standing at, but the iPhone gives a website's camera only 12 MP (4032 × 3024), and it asks Retake / Use Photo after every picture.
  • The two buttons never scroll away. Upright, they sit along the bottom of the screen; turn the phone on its side and they move to a column down the edge, where your thumb is. The rest of the screen scrolls beside them.
  • 📥 Unsorted line — always there. It says how many places are waiting to be sorted, or nothing waiting. Tap it to open Unsorted. (A photo only goes to Unsorted when there is no site yet where you took it; near a site you already have, it joins that site.)
  • Your last photo — shown large, so you can tell whether it is sharp.
  • Today — how many sites and photos so far, and the nearest site you have already recorded.

To record a site

The usual way — full size:

  1. Photograph with the iPhone's Camera app — the Camera Control button, burst, zoom and flash all work as normal.
  2. In History Scout tap 🖼 From Library, select the pictures (you can select many at once), and confirm.
  3. That's it — nothing is asked. Each photo goes by its own location and camera time: next to a site you already have, it joins that site; anywhere new, it starts a place in 📥 Unsorted. A photo already in History Scout is skipped, and it tells you how many.

The photos also stay in your iPhone's Photos app. Delete them there whenever you like — History Scout keeps its own copies.

The quick way — 12 MP: tap 📷 Capture Site, take the picture, tap Use Photo. Same filing, nothing asked.

Either way, what each place is — and everything else — waits for later.

Every photo is checked as it is saved

History Scout writes the picture's own bytes into a store of its own, and reads them straight back to prove they are there before it says the photo is in. If a photo did not save, it tells you on the spot — "That photo did NOT save on this phone. Take it again." — while you are still standing where you can take it again.

Photographs taken before this was built were carried across the first time the app opened. Any picture the phone could not read that day was left exactly as it was and tried again the next time, rather than written off — which is how three photographs that looked lost came back.

Where each photo is — ☁ ⚠ ✖

Every photo carries a mark, wherever it is shown:

MarkWhat it means
☁In your account
⚠On this phone only
✖Picture lost

Photos go to your account by themselves. There is no button to press: each one goes after it is saved, when the app opens, and again when signal returns. ⚠ simply means it has not had its chance yet — leave the app open where there is signal and the mark turns to ☁.

Sorting what you photographed

  1. Tap the 📥 Unsorted line on the Capture screen, or the 📥 Unsorted chip in Sites. Places waiting to be sorted are listed first, marked To sort.
  2. Open one. At the top it asks Not sorted yet — what is it?
  3. Tap one category tile. Type a name if you like — leave it blank and Unsorted 1 becomes Church 1; a name you already gave it stays.
  4. Tap ✅ Sort it. It is now a site like any other.

Until a place is sorted, Bring in new and Export Everything leave it out — so nothing is ever filed under the wrong category — and they tell you how many are waiting. Its photos can still be Sent for safe keeping. Saving notes on an Unsorted place does not sort it; only Sort it, or choosing a category in its Category box, does.

To add more photos to the same site

Just keep photographing. Anything taken within 300 yards of the site you are working on joins it automatically — the bar at the top always says where they are going.

  • To start a new site instead, tap ＋ New. The next photo begins a fresh place in Unsorted.
  • To change how near counts as "the same place", see Capture Settings below.

Photos brought in from the library

A photo in your library may have been taken miles away, days ago — so History Scout does not stamp it with where you are standing now. Pick several at once and each goes by its own location: photos taken in different places become different places in Unsorted.

  • Each photo is dated by when the camera took it, not when you brought it in, so a day's photos stay in order and the dates in your ArcGIS lists are true.
  • The same photo is never brought in twice. History Scout recognizes one it already has — by the second it was taken and its exact size — and skips it. A photo you deleted can be brought in again.
  • Details shows the File type (JPEG or HEIC) alongside the size and where the location came from.
  • If the photo carries its own location, that is the one used, along with the direction it was facing.
  • If it carries none but its own clock says it was taken in the last ten minutes, it borrows your phone's position now — and says so.
  • Otherwise it gets no location, rather than a wrong one. Trail cameras that write a location of all zeros count as none.

To see which happened, open the photo and tap ℹ️ Details: the line Location came from says it in plain words, and Camera's own clock shows the time the camera wrote into the photo.

Checking a photo before you walk away

  • 🔍 Check sharpness opens the photo already zoomed to full detail. Pinch or double-tap to move around.
  • 🗑 Delete and retake takes two taps: the first arms it, the second deletes the photo and opens the camera again. The retake joins the same site.

The name of a site

  • Tap the bar at the top of the Capture screen to rename the site you are working on.
  • The name belongs to the whole group — every photo already in it and every one you add.

3 · The compass

Photo direction matters for documenting a building: "the north face" means something. History Scout records which way you were facing with every photo, and can burn it onto a stamped copy.

  1. On the Capture screen, tap the Heading tile.
  2. If it asks, tap 🧭 Enable Compass — iPhones require one tap per app launch.
  3. The rose turns as you turn. The line underneath says which source it is using.

If the magnetometer is slow or confused — near a tin roof, a vehicle, an iron fence — tap ✋ Set by hand and drag the needle to the way you are facing. That is honestly as good as the sensor in those places. 🧭 Retry compass hands control back to the sensor.

4 · Your sites

Tap 🗂 Sites. Every site you have recorded is here, newest first, with its category, its photo count, the date, and how far away it is from you now.

  • The chips along the top filter by category.
  • 🔒 marks a sensitive site (see below).
  • Tap any site to open its record.

The site record

The record has three parts: the top, your photos, and the form.

The top

  • Name of feature and Category — change either at any time.
  • 🔒 Sensitive location — turn this on for archaeological sites and graves that could be disturbed. Its position is then left off stamped copies, and exports mark it so you know to keep those files private.

Photos

  • Tap any photo to open it full screen.
  • 📷 Add photo / 🖼 From library add more.
  • A ✨ on a photo means you have added an edited version. The other marks say where the picture itself is: ☁ in your account · ⚠ on this phone only · ✖ picture lost.

Recorded position

  • Latitude, longitude, accuracy, elevation, which way you were facing, and when it was first recorded.
  • 📍 Update to my position replaces the position with where you are standing now.
  • ⧉ Copy coords puts them on the clipboard.

The form — this is your MASTER_Point_Template, in your order, spelled the way your geodatabase spells it: Alternate name, Historic name, Feature type, Year, Source date, Description, Notes, Information, Source 1, Source 2, Evidence, Confidence, DHR number, GIS coords, Feature ID.

  • Fields that carry over from your last record — Source 1, Source 2, Source date, County, Recorded by — are marked, so nothing arrives unannounced.
  • Character counters warn you before a field is too long for ArcGIS, which would otherwise chop it silently.
  • More detail holds the extra survey fields (condition, materials, roof, chimneys, threats, cemetery details) for anyone who wants them. Cemetery fields appear only on cemeteries.
  • 💾 Save Changes saves. Only fields you actually changed are touched, so an edit made on your other device meanwhile is not overwritten.

The rest of the buttons

  • 📦 Export this site (ZIP) — just this one site.
  • 🗺 Show on map — jumps to it.
  • 🗑 Delete site — moves it and its photos to Deleted items, where they can be restored on any device. Nothing is erased.

6 · The map

Tap 🗺 Map. Every site with a position is a pin, colored and marked by category.

  • Aerial, Topo and Street switch the background.
  • 📍 Me centres on you. ⤢ All fits every site on screen.
  • Tap a pin for a photo and the name; tap the name to open the record.

Map backgrounds you have looked at are kept for use with no signal.

7 · More — your account

Your account sits at the top of the ⚙️ More tab. What it shows depends on where you stand.

Signed in and approved

  • Who you are signed in as.
  • Records — backed up, or how many changes are waiting for signal.
  • Photos online — how many of the photos on this device are in your online store.

Send — putting photos online

Records go up by themselves, and so do photos now: each one goes after it is saved, when the app opens, and when signal returns. 📤 Send is there for when you want the lot put up at once, while you watch it happen.

  1. Get on Wi-Fi at home.
  2. More → 📤 Send N photos.
  3. Leave History Scout open until it finishes. iPhones stop web apps sending in the background.

While it runs you see which photo it is on and how much has gone. Every step is named, with a clock running beside it, so you can see it working rather than guess. The phone's own copy of the picture is read first. Google is given 30 seconds to answer a check and one minute of failed retries for an upload; if it still will not go, Send stops and tells you which photo, which step, and why, instead of sitting there looking frozen. Stop halts it yourself; pressing Send again carries on from where it stopped.

A photo counts as sent only when Google confirms the copy at exactly the right size. Your photos stay on the phone as well — Send never removes anything. When it finishes, it tells you how much space you can now free up on the phone (see Storage).

Photos in my account — the album of what is in the cloud

More → Photos in my account shows your pictures as they are in the account, fetched online. Nothing on this page is read from the phone, so what you see is proof of what is actually stored — a photo album of the cloud.

  • It needs signal, because every picture is loaded from your account.
  • Where a photo has an edited version, it shows the newest edit, labeled, rather than only the camera's original.

Deleted items

Nothing you delete is erased. 🗑 Deleted items lists deleted sites and photos with the date they went.

  1. More → 🗑 Deleted items.
  2. Tap Restore beside anything you want back.

It returns on every device.

Access requests — owner only

When someone asks for access, you approve them here.

  1. More → 👥 Access requests.
  2. Each request shows the person's name, email and what they record.
  3. Approve puts their email on the approved list; Decline removes the request.
  4. The Approved list below shows who has access, and Remove takes it away again.

Nobody can reach anything until you approve them — an account on its own opens nothing.

Signing out

More → Sign out. Your records and photos stay on the device; they simply stop being backed up until you sign in again.

8 · More — your computer

This card only does its full job in Edge or Chrome on a computer, because only those let a web page write into folders you choose. On a phone, or in Safari or Firefox, it offers a download per site instead — and on a computer running Safari or Firefox the app says so plainly, and tells you to open it in Chrome or Edge, on Windows or Mac.

Setting up, the first time

  1. Open the app on the computer and sign in.
  2. More → Your computer → 📁 Choose a folder.
  3. Pick a folder, or make one in that window — most people call it History Scout. Allow access when asked.
  4. History Scout creates Photos and Records inside it.

Your own setup uses two folders instead: photos on D:\History Scout, records in C:\GIS_Projects\History Scout. That choice lives under Change where things go → Use two folders instead, and once set you are never asked again.

Bring in new — filing everything on your drive

  1. Make sure the drive is plugged in.
  2. More → Your computer → 📥 Bring in new.

It writes:

WhereWhat
Photos\Category\Site\Old Salem Church 01.jpg, 02, … the originals, exactly as the phone handed them to History Scout
Records\new-sites <date>.csvSites you have not brought in before. Your 17 template columns first, then latitude and longitude as numbers, then everything else
Records\changed-sites <date>.csvSites you edited after bringing them in, to update by hand
Records\photos <date>.csvEvery photo filed, with the file name ArcGIS attaches by
Records\new-sites <date>.geojsonThe same sites with their positions built in, for QGIS or ArcGIS
Records\current-sites <date>.csvWritten whenever the list of sites has changed: every site you have now. ArcGIS reads the newest one to notice a site you deleted on the phone — it flags that site, and never deletes anything

Three rules it never breaks:

  • It never overwrites a file. A sky you fixed in Photoshop cannot be put back by a later run.
  • Only what is new. Each photo and site remembers it came in, on that computer.
  • If the folder cannot be reached — the drive unplugged — it says so and writes nothing at all.

Filing happens by itself now

You no longer have to remember the buttons. While History Scout is open in Chrome or Edge on a computer, new photos and edited photos are filed onto the drive by themselves:

  • shortly after you open the app,
  • every five minutes,
  • and soon after photos arrive from the phone.

The rules are exactly the same as the buttons' — nothing is overwritten, only what is new is written. Two allowances are made:

  • An edit saved less than a minute ago waits for the next round, because Photoshop may still be writing it.
  • An unplugged drive is waited for, not complained about. Plug it in and the next round files.

If the browser wants you to allow the folder again, it waits for your tap — it will never ask on its own.

Check for edited photos

  1. In Photoshop, fix the photo and save it beside the original, named after it: Old Salem Church 01 edited.jpg. A second pass is … edited 2.jpg.
  2. More → Your computer → ✨ Check for edited photos.

It reads your site folders, finds files named after photos it filed, and adds them to those records. Your drive is only read — nothing is renamed, moved or replaced. Where a site has only the one photograph, an edit named simply after the site — Charlie's Hope Plantation edited.png — is recognized too, as well as the longer <original name> edited.png.

From then on the record shows your edited version: thumbnails, the viewer, stamped copies and exports. The camera's original is kept beside it for good, and 🖼 Show original in the viewer flips between them.

An edit made on the computer goes up to your account, even though the photograph was taken on the phone. Before, the original had to be on the same device, so a Photoshop edit made at the desk would have stayed at the desk. It now travels like anything else, and shows in Photos in my account as the newest version.

On a phone, where there are no folders to search, use Details → ✨ Add edited version on the photo instead.

Under "Change where things go"

  • Choose a different folder / Use two folders instead — change where things are written.
  • 📥 Bring in everything again — after changing folders, this fills the new one from the start, writes an all-sites list, and still never replaces a file already there.
  • Full path of your folder (optional) — Windows tells a web page the folder, never its address. Type D:\History Scout once and the photos list carries full paths too, which some ArcGIS tools prefer.

Photo Overlay

  • Add stamped copies to exports — a second copy of each photo with the site, position, date and heading burned in, in a folder of its own. Your originals always go out untouched. Sensitive sites say Location withheld instead of coordinates.
  • Show overlay while viewing — the same stamp, previewed in the viewer.

Capture Settings

  • Keep photos full size — on by default. Saves each photo exactly as the iPhone hands it to History Scout, camera information included, never reduced. Measured on an iPhone 17 Pro Max: the Capture Site camera hands over 4032 × 3024 (12 MP) — History Scout keeps all of what it is given. Turn this off only to save space; photos are then reduced to 2,400 pixels and marked as such.
  • Group nearby captures — photos taken near the site you are working on join it automatically.
  • How near is "the same place" — 50 yards, 100 yards, 300 yards (the default) or a quarter mile.
  • Show distances in — Feet and miles (the default) or Meters and kilometers. It changes everything shown — GPS accuracy, elevation, how far away a site is, the text stamped on photos, and the nearness choices below. Whole feet only: phone GPS is good to several feet, not inches. The ArcGIS lists are not affected: their columns are named for their units (accuracy_m, elevation_ft) so nothing already imported changes meaning.
  • Big button on the — Right (the default) or Left: the side of the hand you hold the phone in. From Library moves to that side, upright or turned.
  • Recorded by — your name, put on every new record.

Categories

Add your own category with a name and it appears as a tile when you sort a place, and as a folder when photos are filed. Categories travel to your other devices. A category with no sites can be removed.

Mills and Old Places are now there as well, added to match the reorganized ArcGIS project. They appear on the phone and on the computer alike, and do not double up when the two sync.

Storage

How many sites and photos you have, how big your photos are in full, the space left on the device, and how much of it History Scout's full-size photos take.

Freeing up space on the phone

Photos you bring in from the library exist twice on the phone — in the Photos app and in History Scout. Once they are safely in your account, History Scout can give its share of that space back.

  1. Press Send first, and let it finish.
  2. More → Storage → 🧹 Free up space on this phone. It says how much will be freed.
  3. Tap Free up. Each photo is checked with Google first; only a photo whose online copy is there and complete has its full-size file removed from the phone.

What stays and what changes:

  • The small copies stay, so every site and picture looks exactly the same.
  • Opening a photo full size, exporting it, or Bring in new on the PC fetches the full-size file back from your account. That needs signal.
  • Photos not yet sent, and edited versions, stay on the phone.
  • The iPhone's Photos app is not touched. To free that space too, delete the pictures there yourself, then empty Photos → Albums → Recently Deleted.

10 · ArcGIS — where the records end up

The phone records it, the computer files it, and ArcGIS is where it stays. One tool carries a round of field work in, and it is built so that it can never undo your own work there.

The project of record

C:\GIS_Projects\HISTORIC SITES MASTER PROJECT is the project you open. It holds 133 layers, none of them broken.

Inside it, one single table holds every historic site: HISTORIC SITES.gdb\Historic_Sites — 124 sites and 199 photographs as it stands. On the map they are drawn as folders by History Scout category — Old Homes, Old Churches, Old Schools, Cemeteries, Archaeological Sites, Mills, Old Places, Other — with a layer for each county inside each folder.

Nothing was thrown away to get there. The 14 old per-county site layers were archived into ARCHIVE 2026-09-16.gdb, each named <name>_premerge, only after every site and every photograph had been proved present in the new table. The old C:\GIS_Projects\OLD_HOMES_PROJECT sits idle and untouched for reference; whether it is ever deleted is a separate decision, and yours.

The tool: Bring in History Scout

The toolbox is Tools\History Scout.pyt, and the tool inside it is Bring in History Scout. Its boxes come already filled in: records from C:\GIS_Projects\History Scout, photos from D:\History Scout, the Historic Sites table, and the Virginia county boundaries. A Dry run box shows you exactly what would happen and changes nothing.

To run it

  1. Open History Scout on the PC and let it file — that is what writes the current-sites list the tool reads.
  2. In ArcGIS Pro: Catalog pane → Toolboxes → right-click → Add Toolbox → Tools\History Scout.pyt.
  3. Open Bring in History Scout, tick Dry run, press Run, and read the messages.
  4. Untick Dry run and run it for real.

A plain report of what it did is written into the records folder as ArcGIS import report <date time>.txt.

What it does, every run
New sitesAdded. The county is worked out from where the point actually falls. Public starts at No.
Changed sitesUpdated field by field
Your own ArcGIS editsKept, and reported — never overwritten. An empty box from the phone never wipes something you typed in ArcGIS; only a box the tool itself filled may be emptied, which means you cleared it on the phone
Sites deleted on the phoneMarked Deleted in History Scout. Nothing is ever deleted. If one comes back, it is marked Active again
PhotographsEvery one is attached — the original and every edited version — with its details: date taken, direction faced, original file name, file date, and where you stood
The main photoThe most recent edited photo becomes the main photo for that place
A new category or countyGets its own map layer, in the right folder, with your symbols: shape by category, color by county, your pin-with-house for homes, icons that size with the zoom, and names in charcoal with a white halo

Two things worth knowing about matching: sites are matched by History Scout's own site ID, never by name, so two places with the same name in different counties — Oakland Plantation in Southampton and in Sussex, 26 miles apart — are never merged. And where you have deliberately recorded two records for one name, as with Round Hill the hill and Round Hill the farm, they stay two records, each pointing at the other.

The pins — where the photograph was taken

History Scout puts the pin where you stood to take the photograph, which is usually the road rather than the building. So in ArcGIS you drag the pin onto the house and save it.

  • The tool then keeps where you put it. It marks the site On the subject, and says so once, in plain words.
  • The county follows the pin wherever it ends up.
  • Where you stood is kept on the photograph (CameraLat, CameraLon), so nothing is lost by moving the pin.

Credit, rights and the watermark

Not every photograph in the collection is yours — some come from the Library of Virginia and other institutions, and some of your own edited copies already carry your watermark. So every photograph carries:

TakenBy who took it · CameFrom where it came from · MayPublish Yes / No / Not sure yet, from a dropdown · RightsNote the conditions · Caption · CameraLat and CameraLon where you stood. Sites carry RecordedBy and PinPlacement.

The rules:

  • Your own work is credited Buddy Faison Photography.
  • Your logo never goes on a photograph you did not take. A credit line for the source goes on instead.
  • Nothing goes on a public map or website unless its May publish says Yes. Anything not plainly yours starts at Not sure yet — your 4 History Scout photographs are Yes, the 195 older ones are Not sure yet until they have been sorted.
  • A copy that already carries your watermark must not be stamped a second time.
  • Watermarking happens when a photograph is published, never burned into the archive copy. The logo files are C:\Users\Buddy\Desktop\LOGOS\Photography Logo Buddy-black.png and -white.png, white or black chosen by how dark that corner of the picture is.

The worked example already in the database is Charlie's Hope Plantation — a ruin with only outbuildings left, whose two photographs are Library of Virginia survey material that you enhanced: the house when it still stood, and the survey notes.

11 · A full trip, end to end

  1. In the field: photograph with the iPhone's Camera app. No signal needed. When you stop, tap 🖼 From Library and bring the pictures in.
  2. At a stop or at night: tap the 📥 Unsorted line and sort each place — one tile and Sort it.
  3. Whenever there is signal: photos go to your account by themselves. At home on Wi-Fi you can open the app and press 📤 Send to put the rest up at once while you watch.
  4. At the desk: open the same web address on the PC, in Chrome or Edge. Your sites are there. Fill in the forms with a keyboard.
  5. Filing: while the app is open it files by itself — photos land in Photos\Category\Site\, the lists land in Records. 📥 Bring in new does it at once if you are in a hurry.
  6. Photoshop: fix what needs fixing, save beside the original as … edited.jpg.
  7. The edit goes in: on the next round, or with ✨ Check for edited photos, your edit goes into the record with the original kept — and up to your account.
  8. ArcGIS Pro: run Bring in History Scout, dry run first. New sites are added, changed sites updated without touching what you typed in ArcGIS, every photograph attached with its details, and the newest edit made the main photo.
  9. Drag the pin off the road and onto the building, and save. The tool keeps it there from then on.
  10. Anything you change later on either device is on the other within seconds.

12 · Where everything lives, and what it costs
On the phoneEvery record and photo, in the app's own storage. Works with no signal.
In your accountRecords, and every photo you have sent. Private to you: only a signed-in, confirmed, approved email can read them.
On your computerWhatever Bring in new has filed, in ordinary folders you chose.
In ArcGISHISTORIC SITES MASTER PROJECT — one table with every site and every photograph, for good. Nothing there is ever deleted, and nothing you have typed there is overwritten.

Google's prices at your scale: records and sign-in are free; photo storage is about 2.6¢ per GB a month past the free 5 GB, and about 12¢ per GB to bring them down. A $5-a-month budget alert is set, which emails you rather than switching anything off.

Sensitive sites keep their coordinates out of stamped copies, and exports mark them so you know those files are not for sharing. Landowner names and contacts are in your records and exports — they never leave your account on their own.

14 · If something looks wrong

What you seeWhat to do
The app shows an old buildMore → Version → Check for updates, then the banner. If it stays behind, Reinstall the app — your records are not touched.
"Waiting for approval"The owner has to approve your email. Nothing is lost meanwhile.
Photos show as "Not sent yet" on the PCThey go up by themselves once the phone has signal. To push them now, open the app on the phone and press 📤 Send; the PC picks them up.
A photo is marked ⚠It is on that phone only and has not been up yet. Leave the app open where there is signal, or press 📤 Send.
A photo is marked ✖That picture itself could not be read. Take the photograph again when you can.
Send stops part wayIt names the photo, the step and the reason rather than hanging. Read that line — usually signal — and press Send again; it carries on from where it stopped.
"That photo did NOT save on this phone"The picture did not write safely, and History Scout says so rather than letting you walk away believing it is there. Take it again while you are still standing at the place.
The computer says to use Chrome or EdgeYou are in Safari or Firefox. Those cannot write into your folders. Open the same web address in Chrome or Edge, on Windows or Mac.
"That folder isn't there"The drive is unplugged. Nothing was written. Plug it in and try again.
Nothing filed by Bring in newEverything had been filed before. Use Bring in everything again only if you changed folders.
An edited photo is not foundIt must sit beside the original and be named after it: Old Salem Church 01 edited.jpg.
No GPS fixGive it a moment outdoors; the Position tile turns green. Diagnostics says whether the phone is giving the app location at all.
The compass reads oddlyMetal roofs, vehicles and fences pull it. Set by hand and drag the needle; it is honest, and it never fails.
