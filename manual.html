What History Scout is

A field notebook for old places. You photograph a homestead, church, school, cemetery, archaeological site or store; the app records where you stood, which way you faced, and when — then gets out of your way. The writing up happens later, at the kitchen table, on whichever device you have to hand.

It works in two halves:
The phone, in the fieldOne tap to photograph, one tap for the category, and you're walking again. No signal needed.
The computer, at the deskThe same records on a big screen with a keyboard, photos filed into folders on your drive, and lists ready for ArcGIS.

Everything you record is kept on the device you recorded it on and, once you sign in, in your own private online store. Nothing is shared with anyone.

It is a web app. There is nothing to install from a store: you open a web address, and on the phone you add it to the home screen so it behaves like any other app. Updates arrive by themselves; you tap a banner.

1 · Getting in

The sign-in page

The first time you open History Scout it shows your splash artwork with three choices. You see this once.

ChoiceWhat it means
Sign inYou have an account. Your records back themselves up and appear on your other devices.
Use without an accountEverything works, but stays on that device only, and is not backed up.
Request accessYou don't have an account yet and want one.

To sign in

  1. Tap Sign in.
  2. Type your email and password.
  3. Tap the Sign in button underneath.
  4. You land on the Capture screen. Near the top it should say ☁ Records backed up.

If you forget your password

  1. Tap Sign in, type your email.
  2. Tap Forgot password?
  3. Google emails you a link to set a new one. It may land in your Spam folder.

To ask for an account (this is what anyone else does)

  1. Tap Request access.
  2. Fill in your name, your email, a password you choose, and a line about what you record.
  3. Tap Send request. Two things then happen: you get an email to confirm your address — tap the link in it — and the owner approves you. Until then the app works normally and keeps everything on that device.

2 · The Capture screen — recording in the field

This screen is built for gloves, sunlight and no signal. Everything else in the app can wait.

What is on it

  • Position — your coordinates and how accurate they are. Green is good, amber is fair, red means no fix yet.
  • Heading — which way the phone is pointing. Tap it to open the compass.
  • Local time — what goes on the record.
  • The bar — says where the next photo will land: a site you're already working on, or a new one.
  • Capture Site — the camera. It sits on the side of the hand you hold the phone in: on the right, unless you choose left in Capture Settings.
  • From Library — a photo you already took. It keeps the location and direction the photo carries — see Photos brought in from the library below.
  • The two buttons never scroll away. Upright, they sit along the bottom of the screen; turn the phone on its side and they move to a column down the edge, where your thumb is. The rest of the screen scrolls beside them.
  • 📥 Unsorted line — always there. It says how many places are waiting to be sorted, or nothing waiting. Tap it to open Unsorted. (A photo only goes to Unsorted when there is no site yet where you took it; near a site you already have, it joins that site.)
  • Your last photo — shown large, so you can tell whether it is sharp.
  • Today — how many sites and photos so far, and the nearest site you have already recorded.

To record a site

  1. Tap 📷 Capture Site and take the photo, then Use Photo.
  2. That's it — nothing is asked. A photo from somewhere new is saved straight into 📥 Unsorted as a new place (Unsorted 1, Unsorted 2…). Take the next one.

That is the whole field routine. What each place is, and everything else, waits for later.

Sorting what you photographed

  1. Tap the 📥 Unsorted line on the Capture screen, or the 📥 Unsorted chip in Sites. Places waiting to be sorted are listed first, marked To sort.
  2. Open one. At the top it asks Not sorted yet — what is it?
  3. Tap one category tile. Type a name if you like — leave it blank and Unsorted 1 becomes Church 1; a name you already gave it stays.
  4. Tap ✅ Sort it. It is now a site like any other.

Until a place is sorted, Bring in new and Export Everything leave it out — so nothing is ever filed under the wrong category — and they tell you how many are waiting. Its photos can still be Sent for safe keeping. Saving notes on an Unsorted place does not sort it; only Sort it, or choosing a category in its Category box, does.

To add more photos to the same site

Just keep photographing. Anything taken within 300 yards of the site you are working on joins it automatically — the bar at the top always says where they are going.

  • To start a new site instead, tap ＋ New. The next photo begins a fresh place in Unsorted.
  • To change how near counts as "the same place", see Capture Settings below.

Photos brought in from the library

A photo in your library may have been taken miles away, days ago — so History Scout does not stamp it with where you are standing now. Pick several at once and each goes by its own location: photos taken in different places become different places in Unsorted.

  • If the photo carries its own location, that is the one used, along with the direction it was facing.
  • If it carries none but its own clock says it was taken in the last ten minutes, it borrows your phone's position now — and says so.
  • Otherwise it gets no location, rather than a wrong one. Trail cameras that write a location of all zeros count as none.

To see which happened, open the photo and tap ℹ️ Details: the line Location came from says it in plain words, and Camera's own clock shows the time the camera wrote into the photo.

Checking a photo before you walk away

  • 🔍 Check sharpness opens the photo already zoomed to full detail. Pinch or double-tap to move around.
  • 🗑 Delete and retake takes two taps: the first arms it, the second deletes the photo and opens the camera again. The retake joins the same site.

The name of a site

  • Tap the bar at the top of the Capture screen to rename the site you are working on.
  • The name belongs to the whole group — every photo already in it and every one you add.

3 · The compass

Photo direction matters for documenting a building: "the north face" means something. History Scout records which way you were facing with every photo, and can burn it onto a stamped copy.

  1. On the Capture screen, tap the Heading tile.
  2. If it asks, tap 🧭 Enable Compass — iPhones require one tap per app launch.
  3. The rose turns as you turn. The line underneath says which source it is using.

If the magnetometer is slow or confused — near a tin roof, a vehicle, an iron fence — tap ✋ Set by hand and drag the needle to the way you are facing. That is honestly as good as the sensor in those places. 🧭 Retry compass hands control back to the sensor.

4 · Your sites

Tap 🗂 Sites. Every site you have recorded is here, newest first, with its category, its photo count, the date, and how far away it is from you now.

  • The chips along the top filter by category.
  • 🔒 marks a sensitive site (see below).
  • Tap any site to open its record.

The site record

The record has three parts: the top, your photos, and the form.

The top

  • Name of feature and Category — change either at any time.
  • 🔒 Sensitive location — turn this on for archaeological sites and graves that could be disturbed. Its position is then left off stamped copies, and exports mark it so you know to keep those files private.

Photos

  • Tap any photo to open it full screen.
  • 📷 Add photo / 🖼 From library add more.
  • A ✨ on a photo means you have added an edited version; a ☁ means it is in your online store.

Recorded position

  • Latitude, longitude, accuracy, elevation, which way you were facing, and when it was first recorded.
  • 📍 Update to my position replaces the position with where you are standing now.
  • ⧉ Copy coords puts them on the clipboard.

The form — this is your MASTER_Point_Template, in your order, spelled the way your geodatabase spells it: Alternate name, Historic name, Feature type, Year, Source date, Description, Notes, Information, Source 1, Source 2, Evidence, Confidence, DHR number, GIS coords, Feature ID.

  • Fields that carry over from your last record — Source 1, Source 2, Source date, County, Recorded by — are marked, so nothing arrives unannounced.
  • Character counters warn you before a field is too long for ArcGIS, which would otherwise chop it silently.
  • More detail holds the extra survey fields (condition, materials, roof, chimneys, threats, cemetery details) for anyone who wants them. Cemetery fields appear only on cemeteries.
  • 💾 Save Changes saves. Only fields you actually changed are touched, so an edit made on your other device meanwhile is not overwritten.

The rest of the buttons

  • 📦 Export this site (ZIP) — just this one site.
  • 🗺 Show on map — jumps to it.
  • 🗑 Delete site — moves it and its photos to Deleted items, where they can be restored on any device. Nothing is erased.

5 · The photo viewer

Tap any photo to open it full screen.

ButtonWhat it does
🏷 Show / 🚫 Hide overlayThe site name, position, date and a north arrow, drawn over the photo. It is never burned into your stored photo.
🖼 Show originalAppears when you have added an edited version. Flips between the camera's file and your edit.
ℹ️ DetailsEverything recorded about that photo — including Location came from and Camera's own clock — and ✨ Add edited version.
↔ MoveFiles the photo under a different site.
🗑Moves the photo to Deleted items.

Zooming: pinch to zoom, or double-tap to jump to full detail — one photo pixel per screen pixel — and double-tap again to come back. Past a certain point the app swaps in the full-size original, so you are judging real sharpness, not a preview.

6 · The map

Tap 🗺 Map. Every site with a position is a pin, coloured and marked by category.

  • Aerial, Topo and Street switch the background.
  • 📍 Me centres on you. ⤢ All fits every site on screen.
  • Tap a pin for a photo and the name; tap the name to open the record.

Map backgrounds you have looked at are kept for use with no signal.

7 · More — your account

Your account sits at the top of the ⚙️ More tab. What it shows depends on where you stand.

Signed in and approved

  • Who you are signed in as.
  • Records — backed up, or how many changes are waiting for signal.
  • Photos online — how many of the photos on this device are in your online store.

Send — putting photos online

Records go up by themselves; they are tiny. Photos are large, so they go only when you say so.

  1. Get on Wi-Fi at home.
  2. More → 📤 Send N photos.
  3. Leave History Scout open until it finishes. iPhones stop web apps sending in the background.

While it runs you see which photo it is on and how much has gone. Stop halts it; pressing Send again carries on from where it stopped.

A photo counts as sent only when Google confirms the copy at exactly the right size. Your photos stay on the phone as well — Send never removes anything.

Deleted items

Nothing you delete is erased. 🗑 Deleted items lists deleted sites and photos with the date they went.

  1. More → 🗑 Deleted items.
  2. Tap Restore beside anything you want back.

It returns on every device.

Access requests — owner only

When someone asks for access, you approve them here.

  1. More → 👥 Access requests.
  2. Each request shows the person's name, email and what they record.
  3. Approve puts their email on the approved list; Decline removes the request.
  4. The Approved list below shows who has access, and Remove takes it away again.

Nobody can reach anything until you approve them — an account on its own opens nothing.

Signing out

More → Sign out. Your records and photos stay on the device; they simply stop being backed up until you sign in again.

8 · More — your computer

This card only does its full job in Edge or Chrome on a computer, because only those let a web page write into folders you choose. On a phone, or in Safari or Firefox, it offers a download per site instead.

Setting up, the first time

  1. Open the app on the computer and sign in.
  2. More → Your computer → 📁 Choose a folder.
  3. Pick a folder, or make one in that window — most people call it History Scout. Allow access when asked.
  4. History Scout creates Photos and Records inside it.

Your own setup uses two folders instead: photos on D:\History Scout, records in C:\GIS_Projects\History Scout. That choice lives under Change where things go → Use two folders instead, and once set you are never asked again.

Bring in new — filing everything on your drive

  1. Make sure the drive is plugged in.
  2. More → Your computer → 📥 Bring in new.

It writes:

WhereWhat
Photos\Category\Site\Old Salem Church 01.jpg, 02, … the originals, exactly as the phone handed them to History Scout
Records\new-sites <date>.csvSites you have not brought in before. Your 17 template columns first, then latitude and longitude as numbers, then everything else
Records\changed-sites <date>.csvSites you edited after bringing them in, to update by hand
Records\photos <date>.csvEvery photo filed, with the file name ArcGIS attaches by
Records\new-sites <date>.geojsonThe same sites with their positions built in, for QGIS or ArcGIS

Three rules it never breaks:

  • It never overwrites a file. A sky you fixed in Photoshop cannot be put back by a later run.
  • Only what is new. Each photo and site remembers it came in, on that computer.
  • If the folder cannot be reached — the drive unplugged — it says so and writes nothing at all.

Check for edited photos

  1. In Photoshop, fix the photo and save it beside the original, named after it: Old Salem Church 01 edited.jpg. A second pass is … edited 2.jpg.
  2. More → Your computer → ✨ Check for edited photos.

It reads your site folders, finds files named after photos it filed, and adds them to those records. Your drive is only read — nothing is renamed, moved or replaced.

From then on the record shows your edited version: thumbnails, the viewer, stamped copies and exports. The camera's original is kept beside it for good, and 🖼 Show original in the viewer flips between them.

On a phone, where there are no folders to search, use Details → ✨ Add edited version on the photo instead.

Under "Change where things go"

  • Choose a different folder / Use two folders instead — change where things are written.
  • 📥 Bring in everything again — after changing folders, this fills the new one from the start, writes an all-sites list, and still never replaces a file already there.
  • Full path of your folder (optional) — Windows tells a web page the folder, never its address. Type D:\History Scout once and the photos list carries full paths too, which some ArcGIS tools prefer.

9 · More — exports, settings and the rest

Export

Your insurance policy, and the way to get everything off a phone that has no account.

ButtonWhat you get
📦 Export Everything (ZIP)Photos laid out Photos\Category\Site\, optional stamped copies, and a For ArcGIS folder with sites.csv, photos.csv and sites.geojson, plus a README saying honestly what is inside
📄 CSV onlyOne row per site
🌐 GeoJSONSites with their positions built in

A site's own record also has 📦 Export this site (ZIP).

Photo Overlay

  • Add stamped copies to exports — a second copy of each photo with the site, position, date and heading burned in, in a folder of its own. Your originals always go out untouched. Sensitive sites say Location withheld instead of coordinates.
  • Show overlay while viewing — the same stamp, previewed in the viewer.

Capture Settings

  • Keep photos full size — on by default. Saves each photo exactly as the iPhone hands it to History Scout, camera information included, never reduced. Measured on an iPhone 17 Pro Max: the Capture Site camera hands over 4032 × 3024 (12 MP) — History Scout keeps all of what it is given. Turn this off only to save space; photos are then reduced to 2,400 pixels and marked as such.
  • Group nearby captures — photos taken near the site you are working on join it automatically.
  • How near is "the same place" — 50 yards, 100 yards, 300 yards (the default) or a quarter mile.
  • Capture button on the — Right (the default) or Left: the side of the hand you hold the phone in. Capture moves to that side, upright or turned.
  • Recorded by — your name, put on every new record.

Categories

Add your own category with a name and it appears as a tile when you sort a place, and as a folder when photos are filed. Categories travel to your other devices. A category with no sites can be removed.

Storage

How many sites and photos are on this device, the space they take, and the space left.

Version

  • This copy and On the server — the build numbers.
  • ↻ Check for updates now — asks the server.
  • 🧹 Reinstall the app — throws away the offline copy of the app and fetches a fresh one. Your sites and photos are not touched. Use it only if the app is stuck on an old build.

When a new build is published, a banner appears at the top: tap it to update.

How to use it

Two buttons, 📖 Owner’s Manual and 🧭 Field Card, open this manual and the one-page quick card. They open

inside the app with a Done button, so there is always a way back; they need no signing in, and they are saved for

reading with no signal, like the app itself.

Diagnostics

A plain list of what is working on this device: secure connection, installed to the home screen, GPS, compass permission, whether sensor readings are arriving, storage, the map and export libraries, the offline helper, your account, and whether you are online. Useful when something behaves oddly.

10 · A full trip, end to end

  1. In the field: photograph and walk on — nothing is asked. No signal needed.
  2. At a stop or at night: tap the 📥 Unsorted line and sort each place — one tile and Sort it.
  3. At home, on Wi-Fi: open the app, More → Send, wait for it to finish.
  4. At the desk: open the same web address on the PC. Your sites are there. Fill in the forms with a keyboard.
  5. Bring in new: photos land in Photos\Category\Site\, the lists land in Records.
  6. Photoshop: fix what needs fixing, save beside the original as … edited.jpg.
  7. Check for edited photos: your edit goes into the record, the original kept.
  8. ArcGIS Pro: import new-sites <date>.csv — it leads with your 17 template columns — then attach photos using the photos list, which names each file.
  9. Anything you change later on either device is on the other within seconds.

11 · Where everything lives, and what it costs
On the phoneEvery record and photo, in the app's own storage. Works with no signal.
In your accountRecords, and every photo you have sent. Private to you: only a signed-in, confirmed, approved email can read them.
On your computerWhatever Bring in new has filed, in ordinary folders you chose.

Google's prices at your scale: records and sign-in are free; photo storage is about 2.6¢ per GB a month past the free 5 GB, and about 12¢ per GB to bring them down. A $5-a-month budget alert is set, which emails you rather than switching anything off.

Sensitive sites keep their coordinates out of stamped copies, and exports mark them so you know those files are not for sharing. Landowner names and contacts are in your records and exports — they never leave your account on their own.
